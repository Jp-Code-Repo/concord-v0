# IAM - Access Model

IAM owns identity, authentication, sessions, and effective access. Business services own business data (for example, HR owns employee records).

The authoritative record for authorization decisions is [[iam-authorization-decisions]]. If a historical note or implementation comment conflicts with that record, do not silently choose a behavior. Reconcile the conflict and obtain approval for unresolved decisions before changing behavior.

## The catalog model

Application → Group → Block.

- Applications declare their catalog of Groups and Blocks.
- Groups are organizational containers.
- Blocks are the smallest grantable units.
- A newly declared Block starts with nobody holding it unless access is explicitly configured or granted under an approved rule.
- Do not assume that every Group must have a `view` Block or that every edit/action Block automatically depends on View. Any such dependency must be explicitly defined and approved.

## Institutional user classification

Concord models people in the institution first as **employees**. An employee has an employment category, and may also hold an office leadership assignment:

- **Employee** is the common institutional identity for both staff and teachers.
- **Staff** is one employee category.
- **Teacher** is another employee category.
- **Head of Office** is an office assignment that may be held by either a Staff member or a Teacher. It is not a mutually exclusive employee category.

Conceptually, the relationship is:

```text
Employee
├── Staff
│   └── may be Head of Office
└── Teacher
    └── may be Head of Office
```

This classification describes the institution's people and organizational roles. It must not be confused with IAM authorization profiles: being Staff, Teacher, or Head of Office does not by itself grant every application or Block. Effective access remains governed by the approved IAM access model. A global IT administrator is an IAM administrative access profile, not a replacement for the person's employee category.

This section documents the owner's stated conceptual model. It does not, by itself, approve database schema changes or define how HRIS and IAM will store or synchronize these attributes.

## Who decides what

| Authority | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| HR | Office placement and office-head assignment |
| Office Head | Delegates eligible Blocks to staff within their own office, subject to `can_delegate` |
| IT | Manages the application/catalog rules and global IT administrator access |

IT administrators have global application access under the current model. Granting a Block remains subject to the configured delegation restrictions and audit requirements.

## Effective access

- **IT administrators:** Have global access across applications and are not restricted to a particular office.
- **Office Heads:** Receive access only to the Blocks explicitly configured for their office in `office_head_blocks`, limited to applications assigned to that office. They do not automatically receive every Block.
- **Delegation:** The `can_delegate` flag controls whether an Office Head may delegate a configured Block to staff within their office. A value of `false` prevents Office Head delegation; it does not remove the Office Head's own configured access. It must not restrict IT Administrators. IT Administrators may grant a Block regardless of `can_delegate`, because they own and administer the access policy.
- **Staff:** Receive access from active grants attached to their active office memberships.
- **Office boundaries:** Office Heads manage access and staff within their own office. Access granted in one office must not leak into another office.

### Multiple offices and active office context

The project history contains conflicting decisions about whether a user's authorized access is combined across offices or whether the currently selected office is an active runtime context that affects authorization and audit records.

**This behavior is unresolved.** Do not treat either model as approved until the decision is explicitly confirmed in `iam-authorization-decisions.md`. In particular, do not implement office switching, authorization scoping, or audit-context changes based only on this section.

## Records are not deleted to hide history

- **Accounts:** Disabling an account prevents normal access and invalidates its sessions under the current implementation.
- **Grants:** Grant and revocation metadata are retained (`granted_by/at`, `revoked_by/at`, and `revoke_reason` where supported). Re-granting creates a new row.
- **Memberships:** Memberships are ended using `ended_at`; ending one affects that office's grants.
- **Audit logs:** Authorization changes record the actor and relevant office context where supported.

## Tables

Current IAM tables include:

`users`, `auth_sessions`, `apps`, `app_groups`, `blocks`, `offices`, `office_apps`, `memberships`, `grants`, and `audit_logs`.

The configured Office Head Block model also uses `office_head_blocks`.

## Deferred IAM responsibility: `/api/iam/me` frontend contract

**Status: Deferred. Owner: IAM. Do not block unrelated frontend work on this item.**

- Confirm the authoritative response schema for `access.offices[].apps`, `access.offices[].blocks`, and the office-head indicator (`is_head`).
- Confirm whether the returned Block list represents effective access and whether its entries are unique, so the frontend can safely derive Block counts.
- Confirm the stable distinction between a global IT administrator and an authenticated user who has not been assigned access, including the intended meanings of `access.all_access`, `access.apps`, and `access.offices`.

These are IAM-owned contract/documentation tasks, not a request for the frontend to repeat its investigation or infer missing behavior. The code snapshot and frontend handoff are existing evidence, but they do not by themselves constitute formal contract approval. Revisit this item when IAM work resumes; until then, leave it deferred and avoid silently changing frontend assumptions or redesigning the access model.
